Systematic Futures Research

A systematic approach
to ES futures.

Meridian Research studies the CME E-mini S&P 500 futures market. XGBoost models forecast forward returns every 15 minutes; a defined trading policy translates those forecasts into long, short or flat exposure. The research tests whether those decisions add value beyond passive market exposure.

01 Overview

From market data to forecasts, decisions and evidence.

The pipeline combines ES price, volume, volatility and session information with ZN Treasury futures context. It builds inputs from information available at each decision, trains return forecasts on earlier history, and replays the resulting positions with explicit trading costs. Development, chronological selection and holdout evaluation answer different questions about the system.

02 Methodology

From Data to Trading Decisions

Three connected stages turn observed market data into forecasts and a repeatable trading policy.

Phase 1

Market Features

The system uses CME ES and ZN market data. Features combine minute-bar detail, 15-minute observations, completed hourly bars and prior sessions.

Output Price, Volume & Session Context
Purpose Inputs available at decision time
Phase 2

Return Forecasts

Three XGBoost models are fitted per fold and their forecasts are averaged. The target is the forward log return over four decision steps, nominally one hour.

Output One Forward-Return Score
Purpose Estimate the next hour’s return
Phase 3

Trading Policy

A rolling percentile policy enters long from flat and can reverse between long and short. Session rules and holding limits close exposure to flat.

Output Long / Short / Flat
Purpose Defined entry, reversal and exit rules
03 Exposure & Costs

Defined exposure rules. Explicit trading costs.

Research evaluates a one-contract ES strategy separately from account sizing. The default account replay uses whole MES contracts with a 1× notional exposure target and an 8× entry cap. These settings govern position size; losses still depend on market moves and when the policy exits.

Chronological Scorecard

Select features and policy using only history before each forward year, then replay that year. If no policy qualifies, the incumbent is used and that year is labelled non-blind.

Frozen-Line Holdout

Freeze a configuration before inspecting untouched outcomes. Holdout is a one-use go/no-go check for that line; earlier long-only results do not validate the current reversal policy.

05 Understanding the System

Two Ways to Explore the Research

Start with the plain-language overview, or explore the model, trading policy and evaluation process in more detail.

One market. Three possible positions.

The system studies whether forecasts can help time exposure to the S&P 500 futures market. Long means exposure to rising prices, short means exposure to falling prices, and flat means no position. It starts with a long entry, can reverse direction as forecast rankings change, and closes for session or holding limits.

06 Questions

Common Questions

What is the research trying to establish?

The objective is predictive value beyond passive market exposure. The research compares the strategy with passive long exposure and examines timing, costs and results by direction. Profit in a rising market alone does not establish an edge.

What markets do you cover?

The current research trades ES, the CME E-mini S&P 500 future. ZN, the 10-year Treasury note future, supplies context and is not traded by the strategy. Account replay can size the ES decision stream using ES or Micro E-mini S&P 500 (MES) contract terms; the default uses MES.

What is walk-forward validation?

The model trains on earlier history and predicts a later period. The current setup uses expanding training history, six-month forward folds and a one-day embargo, with purging where labels cross a split. Repeatedly using those results to choose a system still makes them development evidence; Scorecard and holdout address selection and final evaluation separately.

How does account sizing work?

The default replay starts with $100,000 of simulated capital and a 1× notional target in whole MES contracts, subject to an 8× entry cap. Sizing is locked until exit or reversal. This is a research assumption, not a minimum deposit recommendation or a fixed percentage loss limit. Small accounts are constrained by whole-contract sizing.
